# Public Reviewer Guide

This guide offers a transparent pathway for examining the evidence. It does not claim equivalence with formal academic peer review, and review does not imply endorsement.

## What to examine

A useful finding identifies:

- the Claim ID or exact statement being reviewed;
- the source file and page examined;
- what the evidence directly supports;
- what it does not support;
- alternative explanations or contrary evidence;
- uncertainty, redaction, missing dependencies, or source limitations;
- a recommended revision, clarification, or additional review step.

## Review lenses

### Election administration or law

Examine procedural assumptions, state-specific rules, legal terminology, evidentiary gates, and the distinction between application-level misconduct, registration status, ballot activity, counted votes, and certified results.

### Intelligence and structured analysis

Examine source quality, originating versus derivative reporting, confidence language, collection gaps, alternative hypotheses, transferability, and the difference between capability, intent, activity, and demonstrated effect.

### Methodology and reproducibility

Examine provenance, selection and exclusion rules, file integrity, reproducibility, the human-AI audit trail, selection effects, and the search for contrary or superseding evidence.

### Narrative and accessibility

Identify where the explanation becomes inaccessible, repeats itself, obscures the evidence, or moves faster than a general reader can reasonably follow.

### Bidirectional political skepticism

Apply the same burden of proof in every political direction. Identify language that minimizes strong evidence, dignifies weak evidence, preselects a conclusion, or travels farther than the record permits.

## Honesty boundary

AI-assisted analysis is a structured stress test, not independent human endorsement. Named reviewers should be identified only with permission, and their expertise, conflicts, evidence examined, and actual review scope should be stated accurately.

## Review disposition language

A review may recommend:

- supported as written;
- supported with narrower wording;
- context required;
- unresolved;
- contrary evidence required;
- specialist review required;
- revise or withdraw.

**The goal is clarity, not victory.**
