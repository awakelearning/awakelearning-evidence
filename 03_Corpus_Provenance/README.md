# Corpus Provenance, Acquisition, and Preservation

## Release authority and acquisition

The four collections were downloaded by Thomas Richard Annis from the White House Election Integrity page. The public release occurred on July 16, 2026, and the four original archives were downloaded that day.

The unchanged archives were received by the ChatGPT conversation file system at:

- **2026-07-17 02:38:50.517831 UTC**
- **July 16, 2026, 10:38:50.517831 p.m. EDT**

This timestamp establishes that acquisition occurred no later than that moment. The browser download-completion timestamp was not independently retained, so the preservation timestamp is not represented as the exact browser download event.

## Inclusion and exclusion rule

Every readable PDF present in the four supplied archives was included. Duplicate macOS resource-fork files, `.DS_Store`, and non-evidentiary archive-support artifacts were excluded.

The governed corpus contains:

- 58 PDF placements
- 269 review pages
- 55 unique PDF files
- 3 intentional cross-folder repeats

No included source PDF filename was changed. Folder organization was added only to support review navigation. No source PDF content was rewritten, repaired, rotated, or otherwise modified for inclusion.

## What the count means

“58 PDF placements” does not mean 58 independent originating sources. Some files repeat, summarize, quote, or derive from common reporting. Document count must not be treated as independent corroboration.

## Text extraction and rendered-page review

Text extraction and OCR-like processing may be used to locate language, compare dates, and navigate the corpus. They are not final authority where pages are redacted, image-based, or imperfectly extracted. Publication-level claims should return to the rendered source page.

## Integrity records

SHA-256 hashes for the four original ZIP archives and all 58 included PDF placements are preserved in `07_Audit_Trail`. These records allow reviewers to confirm that the files being examined are the files included in the governed corpus.

## Selection and completeness boundary

This is a release-defined corpus. The releasing authority determined which records were made public. The repository can test what the released files support, what they do not support, and how they were framed. It cannot establish that the release is complete, representative of all government holdings, or free from selection effects.

The corpus is evidence of what was released. It is not evidence that nothing relevant exists outside the release.

## Change control

Future additions, removals, replacements, or superseding records should be documented with the date, reason, responsible person, source location, and integrity information. Meaningful changes should remain visible in the revision record.
